## 0. What M4.5 is, and what it is not

**Is** (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN): policy evaluation at request time and re-check at execution; approval creation, decision endpoints, expiry sweep; self-approval denial; the approver queue UI with the `self_requested` flag; approval notifications; audit with approval references. **Acceptance (PRD §3.6, §4.6):** a production operation under a policy cannot execute without an approval; a requester cannot approve their own request unless the policy explicitly allows it; an expired approval window cancels rather than lingering as live production intent; an approved restart creates an operation record that then runs.
**Is not:** deployment approvals (M5.4 reuses the table and the rules; no deployment exists yet), more than one required approval (D3), an editor for approval policies (D13), the history and audit APIs (M4.6), the Azure adapter (deferred, `OPEN-92`).
**The sentence to hold on to afterwards.** After M4.2 a gated operation was *refused*; after M4.5 it is *queued for a person*. Like everything in Phase 4 it is proven against a stubbed provider only until the owner's manual AWS check (`PROGRESS.md` issue 146).

## 1. Findings that shaped the decisions

1. **The seeded policy cannot be reached by a small team.** `allow_self_approval` is `false` in the seed, the Administrator role deliberately lacks `operation:approve`, and no endpoint edits a policy. A one-person or two-person team therefore cannot approve its own production restart. That is the PRD's separation of duties working as designed; whether it fits this owner's estate is the owner's call. (D6, D13)
2. **A production `stop` needs approval with no policy row at all** (SECURITY §16; the evaluator's `PRODUCTION_STOP`). Without a policy there are no `required_approvals`, no window and no self-approval flag to apply. (D3)
3. **The deadline clock would kill a waiting request.** `deadline_at` is `created_at` + the operation timeout (300 s default) and includes queue wait; an approval window is up to an hour. (D4)
4. **The single in-flight slot is held while a request waits** (`AWAITING_APPROVAL` is in `IN_FLIGHT`). That is correct (two pending restarts of one service are one intent too many) but it means an expired or cancelled request must release it promptly. (D4, D8)
5. **`operations.approval_status` has no value for "expired"** (`NOT_REQUIRED | PENDING | APPROVED | REJECTED`) while `approvals.decision` does. (D2)
6. **The worker's current approval check is a flag.** `approval_status != 'APPROVED'` is refused, but nothing proves a person approved; a row edited in SQL would pass. Mandatory rule 3 says approval is *re-checked by the worker*; M4.5 must make the check read evidence. (D7)
7. **A decision races with a cancel, a second decision and the expiry sweep.** Three writers, one row. (D5, D8)
8. **`required_role` has no cheap enforcement:** `Grant` carries no role name. (D6)
9. **"Approval notifications" has no machinery:** the notification outbox is keyed on alert rows, and approvals are not alerts. (D9)
10. **The contract names the audit actions `OPERATION_APPROVED` / `_REJECTED`; the catalogue (§28) says `SERVICE_OPERATION_APPROVED` / `_REJECTED`.** The catalogue is what M4.2 used. (D11)

## 2. Decisions (closed, recommended option taken)

### D1 — `OPEN-93` Scope
Built: migration `0012` (the `approvals` table and `EXPIRED` on `operations.approval_status`); §17.1 creating `AWAITING_APPROVAL` rows; §22.1 – §22.4; the worker re-check from evidence; the expiry sweep (in the existing reaper); approver notification; the approver queue and a decision dialog; the operation page's approval state. *Rejected:* building deployments' approval endpoints now (no deployment exists to test them against — the mocked-data pattern).

### D2 — `OPEN-94` Storage: one `approvals` row per request, and `EXPIRED` on the operation
The table is DATABASE §7 as written (`operation_type`, `operation_id`, `approval_policy_id`, `requested_by`, `approved_by`, `decision`, `reason`, `expires_at`, `created_at`, `decided_at`, with `ck_approvals_decided_consistent`), **one row per request**, created `PENDING` in the request's transaction; deciding it sets `decision`, `approved_by` (the decider, also for a rejection), `reason` and `decided_at`. Added: a partial unique index making **one open request per operation** a storage guarantee (`UNIQUE (operation_type, operation_id) WHERE decision = 'PENDING'`), and `operations.approval_status` gains **`EXPIRED`** (a request nobody decided, or one withdrawn by a cancel). `approved_by` also records a rejecter; the column keeps its documented name. *Rejected:* a row per decision (the same shape would be right for multiple approvers, which D3 declines to build).

### D3 — `OPEN-95` Policy resolution; one approval only
The policy in force is the one the service's action policy links (`applies_to = SERVICE_OPERATION`, environment matching or `*`). **A production `stop` with no policy, or a policy that cannot be read or does not apply, uses the built-in defaults:** one approval, no self-approval, role `APPROVER`, 3 600 s (the seeded values), `approval_policy_id` left `NULL` on the row. The evaluator is unchanged (it already answers *required* on every doubt). **A policy with `required_approvals > 1` is refused at request time (`503 not_configured`, reason `approval_policy_unsupported`)**, fail closed, because the schema allows it and M4.5 would otherwise silently treat "two people must agree" as "one will do" (`OPEN-10`'s working default is one). *Rejected:* quietly clamping to one.

### D4 — `OPEN-96` The request path, the clock and the slot
After the existing checks (permission, idempotent replay, adapter and capability) and the rate limit and in-flight guard, *required* creates: the operation `AWAITING_APPROVAL` with `approval_status = PENDING`, and its `approvals` row, `expires_at = now + approval_window_seconds`, in one transaction with the `SERVICE_OPERATION_REQUESTED` audit row. While waiting, **`deadline_at = expires_at`** (the row's only deadline is its approval window; the reaper does not touch `AWAITING_APPROVAL`). On approval, `deadline_at` is recomputed as `decided_at + operation_timeout_seconds`, so the execution gets the full timeout. A reason is required (the evaluator already says so). §17.1 answers `202` with `execution_status: AWAITING_APPROVAL` and the `approval` summary (the stub `ApprovalSummary` becomes real); nothing is enqueued. The `503 approval_workflow_unavailable` refusal and its code are removed. **A waiting request holds the target's single in-flight slot** (finding 4); its requester or an approver can cancel it (D8). A replay of the same key returns the original `AWAITING_APPROVAL` operation.

### D5 — `OPEN-97` Deciding: order, one transaction, three races
§22.3 / §22.4 check, in order: session; `operation:approve` (the route dependency); the operation exists and is visible to the caller (`404` otherwise, never `403`); it is `AWAITING_APPROVAL` with a `PENDING` approval (`409 invalid_state_transition` otherwise; `approval_expired` as `details.reason` when the window has passed, with the sweep's effect applied); **scope**: `permits("operation", "approve", server_target)` on the stored environment; **separation of duties and role** (D6); a reject needs a reason (`422`). The decision is **one transaction**: a guarded `UPDATE approvals … WHERE id = :id AND decision = 'PENDING' AND expires_at > now() RETURNING`, then the operation's guarded transition (`AWAITING_APPROVAL → APPROVED` or `REJECTED`, `approval_status` set, `deadline_at` reset on approval), the audit row, and the pending-approval alert's resolution. A loser of any race (two deciders, a decision against a cancel or the sweep) sees an empty `RETURNING` and gets `409`, never a false success. On approval the execution task is enqueued **after the commit**; the healer already re-enqueues an unclaimed `APPROVED` row, so a lost enqueue costs seconds, not the operation.

### D6 — `OPEN-98` Separation of duties, scope and role
Self-approval is decided by **one tested pure function** `may_decide(requester, decider, policy, grants)`: the decider may not be the requester unless the policy row says `allow_self_approval` (the built-in default says no). A refusal is `403`, audited `SERVICE_OPERATION_DENIED` with `reason = self_approval_denied`, and the queue marks the row `self_requested` so the UI can disable the button first (never the control). **Scope** is the existing matcher over the target server. **`required_role`** is enforced by a direct query — the decider holds an unexpired assignment of the role named by the policy (default `APPROVER`) — **in addition to** `operation:approve` in scope, because `Grant` carries no role name and the two checks together fail closed. The known looseness is written down: the role assignment and the scope are checked separately, so a role held only in one scope plus the permission held in another would pass; with the seeded roles this cannot happen, and it is issue-listed. *Rejected:* adding the role name to `Grant` (a change to M1.4 authorisation code for a policy column nobody has varied).

### D7 — `OPEN-99` The worker re-checks evidence, not a flag
For a row the evaluator says *requires* approval, the worker (after the claim, before any provider call, and in the resumer) requires **all** of: an `approvals` row for the operation with `decision = 'APPROVED'`, `decided_at <= expires_at`, `approved_by` set; that decider is not the requester unless the policy allows it; the decider is `ACTIVE`, still holds `operation:approve` over the row's stored environment and server, and still holds the required role. Anything else is `FAILED` / `APPROVAL_REQUIRED` with a closed `reason_code` (`approval_missing`, `approval_not_approved`, `approver_revoked`, `self_approval`, `approval_expired`), zero provider calls, audited as a denial and (because it is a denial) not alerted. `approval_status = 'APPROVED'` on the row alone is **no longer sufficient**; the adversarial test edits a row to `APPROVED` in SQL and expects a refusal. A row that does not need approval is unchanged.

### D8 — `OPEN-100` Expiry and cancel
The reaper (30 s, `maintenance`) gains one more query: `PENDING` approvals past `expires_at` become `EXPIRED`, their operation `AWAITING_APPROVAL → CANCELLED` (`approval_status = EXPIRED`, `sanitized_result.reason_code = approval_expired`), an audit row by `system:approval-sweeper` on behalf of the requester, and the pending-approval alert resolves — all in one guarded transaction per row, so a decision that wins the race is not undone. A cancel from `AWAITING_APPROVAL` (the requester, or an approver in scope — already legal) also sets its approval `EXPIRED` with reason `cancelled`, so no open request outlives its operation. `CANCELLED` is a terminal state with no `failure_reason`; the reason is in `sanitized_result`. An expired request frees the in-flight slot immediately.

### D9 — `OPEN-101` Telling approvers: a pending-approval alert, not a new channel
In the request transaction the executor-side alert machinery is reused: a `DCP_OPERATION` alert `ApprovalPending` (`WARNING`) about the target service, through `store.fire` (called from the async session with `run_sync`), which writes the notification deliveries to the **existing channels by the existing routing rules** (environment, severity, target). It resolves on approve, reject, expiry or cancel. Approvers see it on the alert list and in the channels; the approver queue is the place they act. *Rejected:* a second notification subsystem (per-approver email, a user directory an operator cannot read — `OPEN-44`) for a team this size; *accepted cost:* an alert row per waiting request, and approvers must hold `alert:read` (the seeded `APPROVER` does).

### D10 — `OPEN-102` §22.1 / §22.2 shape and visibility
Permission `operation:approve`; rows are filtered **in SQL** to servers the caller may approve on (`readable_servers`-style, by the approve permission). `decision` defaults to `PENDING`; filters `operation_type` (only `SERVICE_OPERATION` is accepted in M4.5; `DEPLOYMENT` is `422`), `environment`, `limit`, cursor (the project's signed cursor). Items carry requester, target, environment, action, reason, `expires_at`, `decision`, and `self_requested`; §22.2 adds `policy {required_approvals, allow_self_approval, required_role}`, `decisions_so_far[]` (at most one in M4.5), and the sanitised target state at request time (the pre-flight state is not read at request time, so this is the service's last observed state and its freshness, labelled). The requester reads their own request through §17.2, which now carries `approval`.

### D11 — `OPEN-103` Audit
Catalogue names: `SERVICE_OPERATION_APPROVED`, `SERVICE_OPERATION_REJECTED` (API_CONTRACTS §22's `OPERATION_*` text is corrected), `SERVICE_OPERATION_DENIED` for self-approval, wrong scope or role, and worker refusals; `SERVICE_OPERATION_CANCELLED` for expiry and cancel, with `approval_id` set on every audit row that concerns an approval (the column exists). Each decision and its audit row commit together. The reason a rejecter gave is audited by its length only (it is user text); it is stored on the `approvals` row.

### D12 — `OPEN-104` Frontend
An **Approvals** page (`/approvals`, linked from the navigation for holders of the advisory `operation:approve`): the pending queue with requester, target, environment, action, reason, time left and a `self_requested` marker; a detail panel restating the four facts; **Approve** and **Reject** (reject requires a reason) in a confirmation dialog; every refusal said in words (`self_approval_denied`, `approval_expired`, `409`, `403`, `404`), the control disabled — never trusted — when `self_requested`. The operation page shows `AWAITING_APPROVAL` with who must decide and when the request expires, the approver and decision time afterwards, and `EXPIRED`. The service page's dialog stops saying approval is unavailable and says "this needs an approval from an Approver; it will wait up to N minutes". `schema.ts` regenerated.

### D13 — `OPEN-105` Deliberately not built
Deployment approvals (M5.4); `required_approvals > 1`; an administrator **editor for approval policies** (they stay as seeded or changed by migration; `APPROVAL_POLICY_CHANGED` stays unused — **if the owner's team cannot separate requester from approver, `allow_self_approval` cannot be turned on from the application**, and that is the first thing M4.5 should be extended with if it matters); per-approver notifications; delegation or out-of-office; approving from the alert page; a bulk decision.

## 5. Security checklist

- Approval is evaluated at request time and **re-checked from evidence** by the worker; the flag on the operation row proves nothing (mandatory rule 3).
- A requester cannot decide their own request unless the policy row allows it; the rule lives in one tested function and is exercised through the API, the worker and a direct-row bypass.
- Every decision path fails closed: an unreadable policy, a missing approval row, a revoked approver, an expired window, a Redis or database error mean *no execution*.
- Scope is the matcher, in SQL for the queue and per row for a decision; an out-of-scope operation is `404`.
- No free text reaches a decision except the rejecter's reason, which is stored, bounded, printable-only and audited by length.
- Tasks still take an id and nothing else; no approval data travels through the broker.
- Every decision is attributed to a human decider; the sweep and the worker are named system actors acting on behalf of the requester.

## 6. Risks

- **Locking out a small team** (finding 1, D13): if one person must both request and approve production work, nothing in the application lets them. Mitigation: stated plainly; the editor for approval policies is the named extension, not a hidden gap.
- **Stranded requests.** A waiting request holds the target's slot until decided, cancelled or expired (at most an hour by default). Mitigation: the sweep, the cancel path, and the alert that tells approvers.
- **A race between three writers** (finding 7). Mitigation: every write is a guarded `UPDATE … RETURNING`, and the tests run the races deterministically, not by load alone.
- **The role/scope looseness** in D6. Mitigation: listed as a known issue with the exact condition; unreachable with the seeded roles.
- **Over-claiming.** After M4.5 the *workflow* is proven against a stub provider. Nothing about AWS is.
- **Touching M4.2 code.** The request path, the worker's `_prepare`, the reaper and `ApprovalSummary` change; their existing tests are the proof the rest did not move.

## 7. What would change this plan

If the team cannot separate requester from approver, add the policy editor (CRUD on `approval_policies`, Administrator, audited `APPROVAL_POLICY_CHANGED`) to this milestone: about one endpoint group, one page and a test matrix. If you want more than one approval required, D3 becomes a row-per-decision design and the worker's evidence check counts decisions. If you would rather not use alerts to tell approvers (D9), the alternative is a second outbox keyed on approvals, which is a migration and a worker more than this milestone budgets.
